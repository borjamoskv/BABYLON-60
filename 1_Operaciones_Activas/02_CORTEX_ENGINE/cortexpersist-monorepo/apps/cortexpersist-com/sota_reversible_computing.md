## 3. Biopsia Crítica

The primary physical mechanism preventing the realization of zero-dissipation reversible computing is the transition from quasi-static to non-equilibrium thermodynamic regimes. In adiabatic CMOS, this is governed by I^2\*R losses in the channel resistance during charging/discharging. In superconducting AQFP, it is governed by phase slips and quasiparticle tunneling. In nanomagnetic logic, it is gyromagnetic damping (Gilbert damping). The fundamental physical mechanism preventing zero-dissipation is the coupling of the computational degrees of freedom (e.g., charge, spin, flux) to the thermal bath (phonons, photons). This coupling is necessary for state stabilization but causes irreversible exergy destruction when the system is driven at finite speeds. Furthermore, logical reversibility does not guarantee physical reversibility unless the control trajectories are perfectly adiabatic, which requires infinite time or infinitely complex, non-dissipative driving potentials.

## 4. Cristalización y Vacío Exérgico

The core unresolved bottleneck is the adiabatic driving overhead paradox. While individual logic gates (e.g., AQFP, adiabatic CMOS) can theoretically operate with arbitrary low dissipation (E proportional to 1/tau), the external power-clock generator (which must supply, recover, and recycle the reactive energy) suffers from high exergy destruction. Current resonant clock generators have a finite quality factor (Q), meaning the energy lost in the clock distribution network is orders of magnitude larger than the energy saved at the logic gates. Until a fully integrated, high-Q (>10^4) superconducting or piezoelectric clock-recovery system is developed that can dynamically match the varying capacitive/inductive loads of a reversible processor, the system-level exergy efficiency of reversible computing will remain worse than conventional irreversible architectures.
